§ 01 • Current Bottlenecks

The Cost of Inaction: Fragmentation, Manual Entry & Delayed Oversight

Uttarakhand's urban health infrastructure is not held back by a lack of clinical commitment, but by severe structural fragmentation across 19 unintegrated portals.

✍️

Frontline Re-Keying Fatigue

ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activist) and ANM (Auxiliary Nurse Midwife) workers re-key the identical citizen into HMIS, RCH, U-WIN, and Ni-kshay due to the absence of a unified ABHA (Ayushman Bharat Health Account) identity layer at registration desks.

📊

Manual Monthly Reconciliation

State Nodal Officers spend the final week of every month manually reconciling conditionality scores, FMR (Financial Management Report) fund burns, and facility KPIs across 19 separate logins, working from month-old stale aggregates.

📑

Unstructured Legacy Notes

Years of UPHC patient encounter notes remain trapped in free-text clinical registries, disconnected from FHIR, SNOMED-CT (clinical terms), and LOINC (laboratory observations), requiring manual re-entry to generate actionable epidemiological data.

📡

Peri-Urban Connectivity Reality

Peri-urban stretches of Dehradun, Haridwar, and Nainital experience frequent bandwidth drops. Cloud-only architectures freeze at the point of care, creating physical OPD bottlenecks.

§ 04 • Phased Rollout

Why This Phasing: Risk-Controlled Sequencing, Not a Big Bang

Structured strictly along ABDM milestones (M1 → M2 → M3). Each stage validates technical and clinical resilience with an explicit go/no-go review before expanding.

PHASE 1 ABDM M1

Pilot: Single District

Dehradun District (34 UPHCs)
  • Why Dehradun: State capital with maximum oversight; diverse mix of urban cores and peri-urban connectivity gaps to test offline-first PWA resilience.
  • ABDM M1 Delivery: Universal ABHA generation and verification at registration desks.
  • Offline Sync Test: Local SQLite queues validated under real network drops in peri-urban stretches.
  • NLP Extraction Sample: Medical NLP model tested on a representative sample of Dehradun's legacy notes before trusting it statewide.
GO / NO-GO REVIEW GATE: Statewide expansion will not proceed until Dehradun pilot proves ≥95% sync reliability and verified clinical note extraction accuracy.
PHASE 2 ABDM M2

Expansion: Two Districts

Haridwar & Nainital (39 UPHCs)
  • Why These Two: Named in the brief for high connectivity variability. Tests plains, pilgrim corridors, and foothill/Kumaon geography.
  • ABDM M2 Delivery: Complete HFR (facilities) and HPR (staff) mapping statewide.
  • Live Portal Ingestion: Turn on live telemetry pipelines for HMIS, RCH, U-WIN, IHIP/IDSP, and DVDMS.
  • Multi-District Load Test: Verifies Kafka sync stability across three distinct district administrations.
MILESTONE CHECK: Operational readiness verified across 73 total UPHCs before Phase 3 onboarding.
PHASE 3 ABDM M3

Statewide Rollout

Remaining 10 Districts (~40 UPHCs)
  • Full 113 Coverage: All 13 Uttarakhand districts live on the Command Centre GIS map.
  • ABDM M3 Delivery: HIP / HIU APIs enabled for cross-facility longitudinal health record exchange.
  • All Feeds Active: Ingestion of PM-JAY/TMS, eSanjeevani, NCD, and Ayushman Arogya Mandir portals.
  • Governance Automation: Automated FMR/PFMS burn rate and NQAS/Kayakalp/Mera Aspataal quality scores.
STATEWIDE HANDOVER: Continuous operations transition to the State Health Society.
PHASE 4 CONTINUOUS

Optimization & ML

Statewide 113 UPHC Fleet
  • Predictive Supply Chain: Machine-learning driven medicine stockout alerts based on seasonal OPD trends.
  • Conditionality Maximizer: Automated scoring tracking every NHM central conditional grant requirement.
  • NLP Continuous Retraining: Iterative accuracy gains on diverse regional clinical handwriting and jargon.
  • Citizen Portals: Integration into state health apps for citizen self-scheduling.
SUSTAINED VALUE: Maximizes annual central NHM conditionality disbursements.
Strategic Sequencing Note: Rather than committing to speculative calendar dates before SDC hosting capacity and district field readiness are formally audited, this roadmap is structured by defined milestones and verifiable technical gates. A verified gate protects state leadership from schedule slippage.

Risk Ledger: Plain Technical & Organizational Candor

Executive leadership trusts proposals that identify risks and define mitigations upfront. Every risk is categorized by who holds the fix.

Technical Risks The state engineering team controls the fix

Sync Reliability Under Weak Bandwidth

Legacy Data Quality & Incompleteness

Mitigation: Pre-migration automated schema validation; dirty records routed to a non-blocking reconciliation queue.

Organizational Risks Needs state administrative action, not code
Frontline Staff Training (113 UPHCs)

Mitigation: Role-based hands-on simulation modules; master trainers in each CMO office; simplified UI in Hindi.

SDC Hosting Readiness & Capacity

Mitigation: Early architectural sizing review with ITDA / SDC teams; staging sandbox deployed ahead of Phase 1 pilot.

Change Management for CMOs

Mitigation: Designate district clinical champions; replace tedious monthly data calls with automated executive email digests.

External Risks Outside the state government's direct control
National Portal API Uptime & Latency

Mitigation: Asynchronous batch polling fallback with circuit breakers; platform never blocks frontline care if U-WIN or HMIS APIs are down.

ABDM Ecosystem Schema Revisions

Mitigation: HL7 FHIR R4 schema abstraction layer in API Gateway isolates internal modules from upstream ABDM version updates.

Vendor / COTS Release Timelines

Mitigation: Use hardened open-source foundations (Kafka, SQLite, Linux) without single-vendor proprietary lock-in.
